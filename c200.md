PR #234 (#196) changes this issue's evidence, and I want the measurement on the record before anyone re-triages.

**PR #207 was closed because on `samples/gmajorblues.mp3` the median tracked interval sat 0.47% from the music while the grid's end-to-end rate sat 1.9% from it, so reading `overallTempo` would have shipped a regression. That ordering was a consequence of #196.** The tracker was inserting about one beat per twelve-bar cycle; the end-to-end rate faithfully summarised the insertions, and the median accidentally resisted them, because a two-thirds detour and its four-thirds catch-up are both off-median.

With #196 fixed, on the same recording:

| | before #196 | after |
|---|---|---|
| the music, from its own loop | 106.000 | 106.000 |
| `BeatGrid.medianTempo()` | 106.556 | 105.469 |
| the grid's end-to-end rate | 108.067 | **106.041** |

**The sign has flipped.** The end-to-end rate is now within 0.04% of the music and the median is 0.54% away from it — which is what #207's central argument said it would be, for the reason it gave: every reader of `estimatedTempo()` places something at `first + k * period`, so what they need is a rate per index, and the median of the intervals is not one.

Measured through the shipped CLI on the #234 branch, forcing each figure with `--tempo` and scoring the emitted chart against the documented progression with `tools/score-chart.py`'s scorer — same chords, same beats, only the bar rate differs:

```
--tempo 105.4688   the median, what ships today          210/313 bars   67.1%
--tempo 106.0408   the grid's own end-to-end rate        267/314 bars   85.0%
```

Eighteen points of printed chart. For comparison, #196 itself moved this recording's chart from 63.9% to 67.1%, because the chart's bar rate — not the beat grid — is what limits it now.

The same shift shows in `ChartLayout`'s drift arithmetic: the estimate is 0.5689 s where the grid ran at 0.5658 s, so a bar line placed by index is about seven beats off the music by the end of twelve minutes. Before #196 it was eighteen beats, and most of that was the tracker; now all of it is this statistic.

**What has not changed:** #207's own caveat that one recording is not enough to choose a statistic, and the dropped-pulse cost it measured — `overallPulseRate` is exactly the mean interval, so a dropped pulse costs one part in `size() - 1`: 5.3% on 20 pulses, 0.05% on 2000. Both still stand. #205 records the statistic that would concede neither.

I am not making this change in #234 — it is `mw-core`, it is a separate serialised review, and #207 already has the implementation and eight rounds of review on `BeatGrid.overallPulseRate` itself. But whoever picks this up should know that the measurement the refutation rested on has inverted, and re-measure rather than inherit either verdict.
