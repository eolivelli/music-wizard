A key and its relative minor share every note of the scale, so the scale cannot separate them. Two things can: a chord on the fifth degree of the minor key whose third is that key's raised seventh, which the relative major does not hold, and the key's own tonic chord, which is worth half again beyond fitting the scale. Where a chord opens or closes the recording is read for nothing at all — on a recording those two spans are a fade-in and a tail decoded from almost no signal.
