sere-doltremare.mp3

"Sere d'oltremare" (iiridio), Italian, 4:59. A cleared candidate for samples/,
staged here until it is confirmed by ear — not a commercial recording like the
rest of this file. Both rights are clear from one source and both were read
first-hand rather than taken from an aggregator: `yt-dlp -J` reports
`Creative Commons Attribution license (reuse allowed)`, and the description
credits `Voce: Carlotta · Musica: Luca, Marco · Testo: Marco, Carlotta, Luca,
Margherita` — the uploader is the band that wrote the words, performed them and
timed them. Found by the #307 survey, which ranked it first for exactly that:
the only candidate whose rights chain is complete with no further step. Fetch
locally, audio and the manual Italian subtitle track together:
  yt-dlp -x --audio-format mp3 --audio-quality 0 \
    --write-sub --sub-langs it --sub-format vtt \
    -o 'uncommitted/sere-doltremare.%(ext)s' https://youtu.be/h1B8Uh07Dik
  tools/vtt-to-lrc.py uncommitted/sere-doltremare.it.vtt --drop-cues 1 \
    > uncommitted/sere-doltremare.lrc
Cue 1 is the title card `~ Sere d'Oltremare ~`, which is displayed and not
sung; dropping it is what keeps the four words out of a transcription score.
The rest is 59 lines and 289 words, and the subtitler's cue *ends* survive the
conversion as bare timestamps, which is what holds each line off the
instrumental stretches around it.

Run 2026-08-11. MW read 97.0 BPM in 4/4 over 481 beats, 143 chord spans, and
Eb major at 14% — a chart circling Eb-Gm-Cm7-Ab throughout. The lyric sheet is
the shape #309 was checked against on generale, and here it holds: the 30 s
intro, the two mid-song instrumental stretches and the 1 min outro all print as
bare chord rows with no lyric line pulled under them. Scored by
tools/score-lyrics.py, which is loop closure and reads zero on both columns by
construction; the row exists so that it stops reading zero.
