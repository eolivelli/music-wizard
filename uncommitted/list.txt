# Recordings held locally: commercial ones for evaluation, and cleared ones
# waiting for an ear.
#
# Most of what is here is a commercial song under ordinary copyright, unlike
# samples/, whose local-only files are at least backing tracks fetched for
# measurement. The audio may not be committed or redistributed, so nothing here
# goes into the committed corpus — but an entry may still carry a fact
# confirmed by ear, and may be scored locally.
#
# Lyrics are not gated. Any lyric file may be fetched and used here as analysis
# input and as ground truth, whatever licence it states or does not state, by
# Enrico's decision and under his responsibility: MW measures how well a program
# reads a recording, it does not perform the music or publish the words. What
# stays fixed is that the text does not leave this directory — everything here
# except this file is gitignored, so nothing is redistributed. A committed test
# or baseline may quote a line or two where it genuinely needs one; the full
# text never is. CLAUDE.md carries the rule.
#
# So an entry here can say what the ear has settled, and some do: what key
# la-canzone-del-sole is in, the complete set of chords it holds, and that
# neither it nor johnny-b-goode holds a minor chord. That is worth writing down
# wherever it is confirmed, because the next reader would otherwise re-derive it
# or, as happened, read MW's correct answer as a bug. A fact stated here can
# also be scored: tools/score-samples.py reads the last two, as seconds of minor
# label and as chart time spent on a chord outside the stated set, both against
# zero. A complete set is not a grid -- it says nothing about when anything
# sounds -- so it prices a chord MW invented where a grid prices alignment.
#
# The second kind of file is a recording whose rights are clear on both sides
# and which is waiting to be confirmed by ear before it is promoted into
# samples/ (docs/phone-to-corpus.md). Its entry says so, and says what the ear
# has still to settle. That gate is about the audio, which promotion would
# commit, and is unaffected by the paragraph above.

vocadito/

Forty clips of solo singing, 7 languages, singers of varying training, recorded
on various devices — with frame-level f0, lyrics, and note annotations from two
independent trained musicians. CC BY 4.0, so unlike its neighbours here this one
could be committed; it is kept local because it is 69 MB of audio the repository
does not need to carry. Fetch:
  mkdir -p uncommitted/vocadito && cd uncommitted/vocadito \
    && curl -L -o vocadito.zip \
      "https://zenodo.org/records/5557945/files/vocadito.zip?download=1" \
    && unzip -q vocadito.zip
  Bittner, Pasalo, Bosch, Meseguer-Brocal, Rubinstein, "vocadito: A dataset of
  solo vocals with f0, note, and lyric annotations", ISMIR 2021 late-breaking.
  DOI 10.5281/zenodo.5557945.

This is the melody stage's real-audio ground truth: `tools/score-melody.py
--source vocadito` scores MW's notes against annotator A1 and prints, beside
them, annotator A2 scored against A1 by the same rule. That second figure is
the point of the corpus. Where a sung note begins is genuinely ambiguous, the
two annotators do not even agree how many notes a clip holds, and their
agreement is the ceiling any melody stage is measured against — a number well
under 100%, which no synthetic package could ever have shown.

Confirmed by ear 2026-08-17: **no minor chord anywhere in the song**. That is

Confirmed by ear 2026-08-17: **the whole song uses A, E and D and nothing

Run 2026-08-11, on the merge of #375. Two errors, both filed:
- **key: E major at 9% confidence, where it is A** (#376). E is the dominant,
  so the chart is written a fifth from home. MW's own spans give E 31.9% of the
  time against A's 26.1%, and only 0.7% of the song is outside E's scale
  against 3.3% outside A's -- so weight and scale fit both point the wrong way,
  and the tell is D at 17.9%: I-IV-V in A borrows nothing, I-bVII-IV in E
  borrows its second commonest chord.
- **tempo: 186.1 BPM, about double** (#378). All three of the independent
  estimators in the verify-tempo skill say ~92.9.
- **the intro is guesswork printed as fact** (#379). Confidence holds at
  0.53-0.71 for eleven seconds, then collapses to 0.03-0.28 where the texture
  thins to voice and light guitar, and the chart prints both alike. 19 of the
  321 spans are at confidence exactly 0, 76 below 0.3.
- **the lyrics are badly misplaced** (#380), with the doubled grid the leading
  candidate: force the rate with --tempo 93 and re-render to test it.
321 spans, 4/4. The vocabulary is right whatever the tonic is called: A, D and
E carry three quarters of the song, which is the progression the ear hears.

https://www.accordiespartiti.it/accordi/italiani/pinguini-tattici-nucleari/islanda/
-- a fan transcription, not authoritative ground truth either, but an
independent second read of the harmony). Its chords, translated from Italian
solfege (DO=C, RE=D, MI=E, FA=F, SOL=G, LA=A, SI=B) and stripped of

worth checking whether that is systematic. This is the best chart-to-
external-reference agreement seen across every recording tried so far.
